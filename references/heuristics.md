# Full editorial heuristic catalog

Use this as a review map, not a list of automatic bans. A feature becomes a
problem only when it harms meaning, evidence, pacing, genre, or voice.
When iterating the skill, this file owns argument, structure, rhythm,
diction, and voice questions — not KEEP examples and not `§N` marker classes.

## A. Purpose and reader value

1. Can the thesis be stated without quoting the title?
2. Does the text solve a problem, explain a mechanism, argue a position, or
   enable a decision?
3. Is the intended reader identifiable from assumed knowledge and examples?
4. Does the draft know what the reader already knows?
5. Is the promised value delivered, not merely announced?
6. Is the scope explicit enough to prevent false universal claims?
7. Does each major section contribute to the reader outcome?
8. Is the requested genre actually present rather than named in the heading?
9. Does the level of detail fit the platform and audience?
10. Is the call to action earned and specific, if one is needed?

## B. Claim quality and factual discipline

11. Separate observation, source claim, inference, opinion, and recommendation.
12. Preserve modal strength: `может`, `часто`, `вероятно`, `показывает` are not
    interchangeable with `всегда`, `доказывает`, `вызывает`.
13. Check that quantifiers have evidence: all, most, many, few, only, never.
14. Check causal verbs for a mechanism or controlled evidence.
15. Check comparisons for a baseline and comparable conditions.
16. Check superlatives and first/only/best claims.
17. Check numbers for unit, period, denominator, sample, and source.
18. Check percentages against raw values when both exist.
19. Check ranges and multipliers for preserved typography and magnitude.
20. Check dates, versions, model names, product names, and status for consistency.
21. Keep source attribution attached to the claim it supports.
22. Do not make a linked page support more than it actually says.
23. Distinguish one user's report from community consensus.
24. Distinguish benchmark behavior from production behavior.
25. Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
26. Distinguish possibility from probability and probability from frequency.
27. Do not add details merely because they sound plausible.
28. Do not invent first-person experience, interviews, reactions, or quotes.
29. Surface internal contradictions instead of choosing one silently.
30. Mark facts that cannot be repaired from supplied material.

## C. Logic and nonsense detection

31. Does each conclusion follow from premises present in the text?
32. Is a missing premise factual, definitional, or merely rhetorical?
33. Does the example instantiate the same concept as the claim?
34. Is an analogy used to clarify rather than prove?
35. Has a local case been generalized to a class?
36. Has correlation been rewritten as causation?
37. Has sequence been mistaken for consequence?
38. Has a term changed meaning between sections?
39. Are two categories actually mutually exclusive?
40. Is a spectrum presented as a binary choice?
41. Is a trade-off described as a universal improvement?
42. Is a recommendation compatible with the constraints named earlier?
43. Is a problem attributed to a tool when process or configuration is the
    actual variable?
44. Does a paragraph contain an actor, event, relation, or testable proposition?
45. After removing prestige terms, does the sentence still say anything?
46. Does the mechanism explain how the effect occurs?
47. Does `поэтому` connect cause and effect or merely neighboring facts?
48. Does `это значит` preserve the original statement's strength?
49. Does the final takeaway contradict a caveat buried earlier?
50. Is uncertainty acknowledged at the exact point where it matters?

## D. Information architecture

51. Give every section one dominant purpose.
52. Keep evidence near its claim.
53. Keep caveats near the claim they constrain.
54. Introduce terminology at first necessary use.
55. Move implementation detail after conceptual orientation when the audience
    needs orientation; reverse for a hands-on recipe.
56. Put the strongest concrete reason before secondary taxonomy.
57. Remove sections that exist only to make the outline look complete.
58. Merge headings with one short paragraph unless navigation needs them.
59. Split sections containing two unrelated arguments.
60. Avoid repeating the same claim in introduction, body, and conclusion.
61. Avoid a prose table of contents after visible headings already provide one.
62. Avoid background history that does not change interpretation.
63. Avoid a definition section for terms the target reader knows.
64. Avoid identical internal templates and card grids across sections (e.g.
    repeated "Как устроено" / "Профит", "Проблема" / "Решение", identical
    bold-label bullets). Break hyper-symmetry; let cases vary in volume and
    dirty detail.
65. Allow unequal section lengths according to importance.
66. Make the center of gravity visible through evidence and space.
67. Use transitions when direction changes, not between every paragraph.
68. Let adjacent paragraphs connect implicitly when the relation is obvious.
69. Keep asides, jumps, returns, and spontaneous rhetorical breathers;
    conveyor-belt syllabus progression with zero organic diversions is an AI tell.
70. End when the argument lands; do not append a universal moral or a stock
    closer such as `Я бы оставил такую схему`.

## E. Paragraph function

71. One paragraph may contain several sentences but should perform one primary
    rhetorical job.
72. Topic sentences need not all appear first.
73. A one-sentence paragraph should earn emphasis, pace, or transition.
74. A long paragraph should have internal progression, not accumulated clauses.
75. Remove paragraphs that announce upcoming explanation without adding stakes.
76. Remove paragraphs that praise the topic's importance.
77. Remove paragraphs that paraphrase the previous paragraph.
78. Remove examples that repeat rather than illuminate.
79. Merge a claim and its one-sentence explanation when separation is artificial.
80. Split a paragraph when evidence, counterargument, and recommendation compete.
81. Do not force every paragraph into claim → explanation → takeaway.
82. Do not end every paragraph with a miniature conclusion.
83. Check pronoun references across paragraph boundaries.
84. Check that `это`, `такой`, `данный`, and `подобный` have clear antecedents.
85. Preserve purposeful callbacks; remove accidental repetition.

## F. Water and redundancy

86. Delete throat-clearing before the first concrete claim.
87. Delete announcements such as `ниже мы рассмотрим` when structure shows it.
88. Replace importance claims with consequence or remove them.
89. Remove meta-commentary about how carefully or comprehensively the topic will
    be discussed.
90. Remove restatements introduced by `иными словами` unless they genuinely
    simplify a difficult idea.
91. Remove paired synonyms that do not add distinction.
92. Remove adjectives already implied by the noun.
93. Remove adverbs already implied by the verb.
94. Remove repeated qualifiers with the same scope.
95. Remove parenthetical disclaimers that can be stated directly.
96. Remove decorative examples with no argumentative role.
97. Remove generic benefits applicable to any product or process.
98. Remove conclusions that merely list preceding headings.
99. Compress copied source language into the exact fact needed, preserving
    attribution.
100. Do not compress necessary procedure, risk, or qualification into ambiguity.

## G. Sentence-level clarity

101. Prefer a visible actor when agency matters. Inanimate nouns should not
     perform human verbs: a complaint does not become a fix, data does not
     tell, a decision does not emerge.
102. Use passive voice when actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally
     backgrounded.
103. Replace stacked verbal nouns with verbs where possible.
104. Break chains of four or more genitives when they obscure relations.
105. Keep modifiers next to what they modify.
106. Resolve dangling participial phrases.
107. Avoid several nested parentheticals.
108. Avoid colon chains that repeatedly redefine the same statement.
109. Avoid comma splices and fragments created by mechanical dash removal.
110. Split sentences carrying multiple independent claims.
111. Join staccato fragments that imitate confidence without reasoning.
112. Preserve a fragment when it creates deliberate emphasis.
113. Prefer a concrete verb over `осуществлять`, `производить`, `обеспечивать`
     plus a noun when meaning stays precise.
114. Prefer natural Russian order over translated English order.
115. Avoid unnecessary demonstratives: `данный`, `указанный`, `вышеупомянутый`.
116. Avoid bureaucratic prepositions: `в рамках`, `в части`, `в контексте` when a
     simpler relation exists.
117. Avoid ambiguous `который` chains.
118. Check agreement after cuts and merges.
119. Check aspect and tense consistency.
120. Keep domain terminology when simplification would lose precision.

## H. Rhythm and variation

121. Variation should follow thought, not a random sentence-length target.
122. Flag runs of similarly shaped sentences; inspect before changing.
123. Flag repeated openings, especially abstract noun + verb.
124. Flag repeated `Это...`, `Такой...`, `Данный...` paragraph starts.
125. Flag repeated colon-led explanations.
126. Flag repeated contrast templates.
127. Flag repeated three-item enumerations.
128. Flag repeated sentence-final slogans.
129. Use short sentences for real emphasis, not every conclusion.
130. Let a complex sentence carry a genuinely layered relation.
131. Avoid alternating short and long sentences mechanically.
132. Preserve deliberate accumulation and parallelism.
133. Break perfect symmetry when it falsely equalizes importance. Uncanny
     volumetric balance across paragraphs or scenario cards signals machine generation.
134. Keep symmetry where comparison or instructions benefit from it.
135. Read aloud mentally: fix places where grammar is correct but breath and
     emphasis fight the thought. Insert spontaneous breathers and cadence shifts
     between dense teardowns and blunt reactions.

## I. Diction and register

136. Prefer specific nouns and verbs over vague `аспект`, `фактор`, `подход`,
     `решение`, `возможность` when the actual object is known.
137. Remove unsupported evaluative adjectives: effective, robust, innovative,
     seamless, powerful, comprehensive.
138. Keep evaluative language when evidence or author stance supports it.
139. Avoid imported business verbs where ordinary Russian is clearer.
140. Keep established technical anglicisms for an expert audience.
141. Avoid mixing formal bureaucracy, marketing copy, and casual slang without
     a reason.
142. Avoid euphemisms that hide failure or responsibility.
143. Avoid consultant abstractions with no operational definition.
144. Avoid accidental grandiosity.
145. Cut fake intimacy (`мы с тобой наверняка`) that carries no claim; keep a
     personal reaction to the actual material.
146. Colloquial / slang is a Pass H tool on stiff copy. Flag only a
     mechanical dump (`Короче` every paragraph) that fights the speaker.
147. Preserve sharpness, irritation, doubt, humor, or dryness; in article/post
     register prefer a visible stance over a dry briefing. Beware antiseptic
     sterility ("вылизанность"): prose without human friction, pet peeves,
     or categorical conviction reads like a consulting memo.
148. Do not normalize every unusual, categorical, or opinionated phrase. Call
     bad engineering ideas bad.
149. Do not upgrade ordinary words to prestige synonyms.
150. Use terminology consistently; do not vary technical terms for style.

## J. Voice and authorship context

151. Infer voice from several decisions, not one recurring phrase.
152. Match density, directness, and distance from the reader.
153. Match how the author handles uncertainty and disagreement.
154. Match tolerance for jargon and explanation.
155. Match relationship to examples: personal, operational, hypothetical, or
     sourced.
156. Do not copy an exemplar's exact hook, metaphor, cadence, or ending.
157. Pass H: 1–3 informal slips in ordinary running prose (spoken syntax,
     crooked agreement, a missing letter). Never in names, numbers, code,
     or links.
158. Do not invent biography-informed anecdotes.
159. Do not force greetings, jokes, questions, CTA, or `P.S.` because they occur
     in other publications.
160. Respect genre differences within one author's work.
161. Prefer the current explicit brief over historical habits.
162. If the draft already has a strong individual voice, edit around it.
163. Distinguish voice from opinion: polishing must not change the author's
     position silently.
164. Distinguish voice from structure: samples do not override requested form.
165. Preserve meaningful roughness; remove only roughness that hides the claim.
165b. Distinguish author telemetry from synthetic precision: when raw terminal
      logs or benchmark counters appear, prompt the author to claim first-person
      agency (`«я замерил на стенде»`, `«в моем логе»`) instead of deleting them
      or leaving them in passive bureaucracy (`«в зафиксированном кейсе»`).

## K. Introductions and endings

166. Start near the actual problem, scene, claim, or surprising evidence.
167. Avoid `в современном мире`, dictionary definitions, and universal history.
168. Avoid announcing comprehensiveness.
169. Avoid a rhetorical question whose answer is the title.
170. Avoid generic stakes such as `это важно для всех`.
171. Establish why this author or evidence adds something.
172. Do not reveal every conclusion before the argument if discovery matters.
173. Do not withhold the thesis merely to manufacture suspense in an expert text.
174. End on implication, boundary, decision, result, or useful next action.
175. Avoid `будущее покажет`, `время не стоит на месте`, and generic optimism.
176. Avoid repeating the introduction in past tense.
177. Avoid adding a question for engagement when no discussion is needed.
178. Keep `P.S.` only if it carries a real secondary note, source, correction, or
     earned joke.

## L. Final calibration

179. Is the result better, or merely more conventional?
180. Is it shorter where the original was padded?
181. Is any useful detail missing after compression?
182. Did uncertainty survive?
183. Did a mild claim become dramatic?
184. Did a technical term become less precise?
185. In article/post, did the edit install `«ёлочки»`, decorative em dashes,
     chopped mini-sentences, or a list that used to be one thought? In
     academic/legal, did source book typography get flattened?
186. Did a useful checklist become a paragraph, or did a fake list survive?
187. Did a useful table become duplicated in prose?
188. Did the edit introduce a new AI pattern while removing another?
189. Can any remaining block be deleted without loss?
190. Would restoring any original phrase improve voice or precision?
191. Did water and calques get deleted, or only renamed (`ключевой` → `важнейший`)?
192. Could this opening or closer sit on any other article after a humanizer pass?
193. Did `==`, `=>`, `->`, or `vs` survive when they marked identity or implication?
194. In article/post register, is a stance visible without a fabricated episode?
195. Is the outline still a bit uneven, or was it combed into symmetry?
196. Would a reader in this genre flag the fill as a chatbot, brochure, or
     wiki-card? House frame and genre formality are not that flag.
197. Did the `Маркеры` table name AI / вода / признак with `§N` or a Pass 3
     span? Did Pass H run without touching protected spans?
